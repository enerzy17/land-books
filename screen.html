What comes back, exactly

  1. Owner of record, mailing address, and the last recorded transfer with date and consideration.
  2. Arrears to the cent, broken out, plus the full statutory purchase total including the $10 assignment fee.
  3. The redemption window with real dates, the notice period, and the earliest date you can file.
  4. Frontage in feet to the nearest mapped centerline, computed from the geometry, plus the road grade: maintained, primitive, or platted only.
  5. FEMA flood zone at the centroid.
  6. The comparable: a live ask or a recorded sale on the same plat, sourced and dated.
  7. The exit arithmetic at 60% of spread with the 2x floor, and the paper version at 12% if you would rather carry it.
  8. One page, PDF, plus the raw county printouts the numbers came from.

Who buys this

Dealers buying Arizona certificates over the counter and reselling on terms. You already know the county will assign the certificate to anyone who pays, A.R.S. 42-18122, no auction and no bidder number. What eats the two hours is the frontage run and the comp. That is the two hours $89 buys.

What it is not

Not a title report, not an appraisal, not advice. Every number traces to a county or assessor printout and the printout comes with it. Stock moves and the county list is a snapshot, so the sheet carries the pull time and you confirm at the window.
